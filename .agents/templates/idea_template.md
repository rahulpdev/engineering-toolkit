## OKR Idea Intake Template

> Copy this whole template into a doc, answer the questions in your own words, then paste the filled-in version below into the LLM.

---

### 1. Idea snapshot

1. One sentence problem

   - In one sentence, what problem are you trying to solve?

2. One sentence hypothesis
   - In one sentence, what is your hypothesis about how this idea creates value?

---

### 2. Users and problem

3. Primary user segments

   - Who are the primary users or customers?
   - Write 1–3 simple descriptions (e.g. “credit risk analysts at fintechs,” “solo productivity nerds using ClickUp”).

4. Core pain

   - What specific pain or frustration do they experience today?
   - Describe real situations or workflows if you can.

5. Current workaround

   - How do they solve this problem today (tools, processes, manual work, spreadsheets, other SaaS)?

6. Severity and frequency
   - How painful is this problem on a typical day or week?
   - How often does it show up?

---

### 3. Outcomes and value

7. Desired user outcomes

   - What should be **different in the user’s life/work** if this OKR succeeds?
   - Examples: less time on X, fewer errors in Y, less cognitive load, more clarity, faster decisions.

8. Desired business outcomes

- What should be different in terms of **your business** if this OKR succeeds?
- Examples: validated demand, qualified leads, revenue, stronger case studies.

9. Candidate metrics

- List any metrics that feel relevant, even if you do not know baselines yet.
- Examples: “minutes per task,” “number of PDFs processed per week,” “weekly active users,” “number of testers who stick with the system for 30 days.”

---

### 4. Scope for this cycle

10. In scope for this OKR

- What do you want to **definitely tackle** in this cycle?
- Think in terms of _outcomes_ or _experiments_, not just features.

11. Explicitly out of scope

- What must **not** be tackled in this cycle, even if tempting?
- Examples: “full multi-tenant SaaS build,” “mobile app,” “full brand and marketing site.”

12. Dependencies and related work

- Are there existing systems, setups, or experiments that this OKR depends on or should reuse?
- Examples: existing ClickUp setup, existing n8n instance, existing architecture from another person’s project.

---

### 5. Constraints and context

13. Technical constraints

- What technical constraints are already known?
- Examples: your skill set, stack preferences, hosting budget, data residency, security/compliance requirements, tools you refuse to use.

14. Integration and ecosystem constraints

- Which tools **must** be integrated, if any?
- Examples: ClickUp, n8n, specific APIs, banking data sources, etc.

15. Non-negotiables

- List any must-haves or “red lines” (e.g., “no storing raw PDFs long term,” “running costs under £100/month,” “must be usable via API,” “must require no additional software for end users”).

---

### 6. Risks, assumptions, and unknowns

16. Biggest unknowns

- What are the 3–5 biggest things you **do not know yet** that could change the shape of this idea?

17. Key assumptions

- Write down your main assumptions as simple statements.
- Examples: “Risk analysts will trust an automated PDF parser,” “people are willing to adapt their workflow to my GTD variant,” “ClickUp API is robust enough to be fully driven via n8n.”

18. Risks and failure modes

- What could make this idea fail or make the OKR not worth continuing?
- Think about tech risk, adoption risk, compliance risk, and your personal energy/time.

19. Early validation signals

- What signals would suggest you’re on the right track **early** in the OKR period?
- Examples: X users agree to pilot, Y hours of manual work removed, Z testers actively using system after 2 weeks.

20. Early red flags

- What signals would suggest you should pivot or stop?

---

### 7. Focus of this OKR

21. Primary focus

- For this specific OKR, which is the **primary** focus? Pick one and explain why.
  - [ ] Learning and validation (prove/disprove a hypothesis)
  - [ ] Adoption and engagement (get people to actually use it)
  - [ ] Efficiency and quality (improve performance or reliability of an existing thing)
  - [ ] Revenue and commercialisation

22. Secondary focus

- Is there a secondary focus that matters (e.g. “build re-usable architecture,” “clarify long-term product direction”)?

23. Trade-offs

- Are there obvious trade-offs you are willing to make?
- Examples: “I will accept manual onboarding to get better learning,” “I will slow down feature build to get cleaner architecture.”
