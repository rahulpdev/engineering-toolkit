# Product Manager template

## Intro

I am a product manager and a project manager, with a non-technical background, with primary responsibility for the product vision and strategy, and the execution and on-time delivery, of a range of software applications.

I manage a team of product managers and software engineers tasked with delivering high-quality software to a production environment, and delivering feature and functionality software updates on a regular release cycle.

## Objective

I need a high-quality template to repeatedly use to enable the delivery of thoughtful, comprehensive briefs to the engineering team for new software development projects.

## Context

The purpose of the template is to assist me in producing a project blueprint that, in turn, is used by product managers to create thoughtful project briefs that, in turn, is used by software engineers to create a comprehensive project implementation plan, all built on future-proofed project design and architecture.

I don't have the skills and experience to deliver thoughtful, comprehensive project briefs and implementation plans. The template must explicitly account for this.

It is not a requirement for the template to capture every detail of the project. I will not have all the relevant information at the outset.

I must rely on my team of product managers and software engineers to think for themselves so that collectively we deliver thoughtful, comprehensive, future-proofed project documentation.

Software will be built and improved iteratively over time so it is critical to future-proof project design and architecture, minmise technical debt, and build and release software in a multi-stage, 'MVP first' implementation approach.

Therefore it is important that the template prompts me:

1. To consider relevant up-front questions.
2. To articulate questions to the product managers to ensure they consider and document relevant topics and questions up-front required for a thoughtful project brief.
3. To articulate questions to the engineering team to prompt them to consider and raise raise relevant technical topics and questions up-front for a comprehensive project implementation plan.
4. To consider and articulate a phased development process, where features and functionality are documented but deferred for future release.

Project briefs will be documented in markdown, using headings, ordered and unordered lists. The tone of each project brief will be short bullet-point style sentences and tightly written paragraphs.

The software applications will range widely in their complexity of features, functionality, UX, UI, scope and scalability.

## Workflow

1. I complete the template to produce a blueprint for a new software development project.
2. My staff of product managers translate the blueprint into a thoughtful project brief that captures details I did not articulate in the blueprint.
3. Project brief is delivered to the engineering team.
4. Engineering team raises relevant technical topics and questions.
5. I consider and produce a response to their queries.
6. Product manager team translates my responses and updates the project brief.
7. Final project brief is delivered to the engineering team.
8. Engineering team produces a comprehensive multi-staged project implementation plan.
9. I sign off on implementation plan and engineering team builds and releases software v1.

## Template design

I must be able to re-use the template across a wide range of projects ranging from simple to highly-complex software development.

The template must have clear section headings and be in markdown format.

The template must be designed as a series of questions that, as I provide answers, naturally builds out and converts itself into a project blueprint.

Once I've completed the template, the final content must explicitly articulate any relevant questions that I need to raise with my product managers and/or engineers in order to direct them to deliver on their downstream documentation. (They have no memory, so I have to raise relevant questions on each and every project.)
